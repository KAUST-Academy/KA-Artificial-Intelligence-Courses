\section{Extensions \& Applications}
\begin{frame}{TD3: DDPG done right \rref{7}}
\vspace{0.3em}
The experiments showed DDPG \emph{overestimates} $Q$ and is brittle. \textbf{TD3} (Twin Delayed DDPG) keeps the deterministic off-policy actor-critic but adds three fixes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \footnotesize
    \item \textbf{Clipped double-Q (twin critics):} learn two critics, use the \emph{minimum} for the TD target to curb overestimation bias:
    \[
    y = r + \gamma \min_{i=1,2} Q_{\theta_i'}\!\big(s',\, \mu_{\theta'}(s') + \epsilon\big)
    \]
    \item \textbf{Target policy smoothing:} $\epsilon \sim \mathrm{clip}(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma),-c,c)$ --- regularizes the target so the actor cannot exploit sharp $Q$ peaks.
    \item \textbf{Delayed policy updates:} update the actor (and targets) less often than the critics (e.g.\ every 2 critic steps) for lower-variance policy updates.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{0.2em}
\footnotesize\textcolor{red}{Takeaway:} TD3 is the deterministic successor to DDPG; its clipped double-Q idea carries directly into SAC (next lecture).
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Beyond TD3: other directions}
\begin{itemize}
    \footnotesize
    \item \textbf{Make the actor an explicit $\arg\max$ of a convex critic} --- avoids a separate policy network entirely:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Continuous Deep Q-Learning with Model-based Acceleration} (NAF) \rref{9}
        \item \emph{Input Convex Neural Networks} \rref{10}
    \end{itemize}
    \vspace{0.3em}
    \item \textbf{Stochastic policy search} --- replace the deterministic actor with an entropy-regularised stochastic one (the Day 6 path):
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Soft Actor-Critic} \rref{8}
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
