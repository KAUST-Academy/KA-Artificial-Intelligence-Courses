\section{Putting It Together}
\begin{frame}{}
    \LARGE CNN: \textbf{Putting It Together}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[allowframebreaks]{Tracing Shapes Through a Small CNN}
    Take a $32 \times 32 \times 3$ input and apply $O = \left\lfloor \frac{I + 2P - K}{S} + 1 \right\rfloor$ at every step.

    \vspace{0.4em}
    \centering
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{|l|l|c|}
        \hline
        \textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Setting} & \textbf{Output shape} \\
        \hline
        Input & & $32 \times 32 \times 3$ \\
        Convolution + ReLU & 32 filters, $K = 3$, $P = 1$, $S = 1$ & $32 \times 32 \times 32$ \\
        Max pooling & $K = 2$, $S = 2$ & $16 \times 16 \times 32$ \\
        Convolution + ReLU & 64 filters, $K = 3$, $P = 1$, $S = 1$ & $16 \times 16 \times 64$ \\
        Max pooling & $K = 2$, $S = 2$ & $8 \times 8 \times 64$ \\
        Flatten & & $4096$ \\
        Fully connected + ReLU & 128 units & $128$ \\
        Fully connected & 10 classes & $10$ \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}

    \raggedright
    \vspace{0.4em}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Padding of 1 with a $3 \times 3$ kernel keeps the resolution: $\lfloor (32 + 2 - 3)/1 \rfloor + 1 = 32$.
        \item Each pooling layer halves height and width: $\lfloor (32 - 2)/2 \rfloor + 1 = 16$.
        \item Depth is set by the number of filters, resolution by padding and stride.
    \end{itemize}

    \framebreak

    \textbf{Where do the parameters live?}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Convolution 1: $3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 32 + 32 = 896$
        \item Convolution 2: $3 \times 3 \times 32 \times 64 + 64 = 18{,}496$
        \item Fully connected 1: $4096 \times 128 + 128 = 524{,}416$
        \item Fully connected 2: $128 \times 10 + 10 = 1{,}290$
        \item Total: $545{,}098$
    \end{itemize}

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The two convolution layers hold $19{,}392$ weights, about 3.6\% of the network. The first fully connected layer alone holds 96\%.
        \item Convolution is cheap because the same kernel is reused at every position. A fully connected layer needs its own weight for every input.
        \item This is why architectures covered later shrink the feature map further before flattening, or drop the flatten step and use global average pooling instead.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
