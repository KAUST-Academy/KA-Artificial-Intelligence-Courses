\begin{frame}{What is \emph{continuous control}?}
\vspace{0.6em}
\textbf{Control:} choosing actions over time to drive a dynamical system toward desired behaviour --- exactly the RL problem on an MDP $(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,\gamma)$.
\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Discrete control:} the action set $\mathcal{A}$ is a \emph{finite} list --- move left/right, one of 4{,}672 Go moves, an Atari button. The policy picks one entry.
    \vspace{0.3em}
    \item \textbf{Continuous control:} the action is a \emph{real-valued vector} $a \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ (often bounded), e.g.\ $\mathcal{A} = [-1,1]^{d}$.
    \begin{itemize}
        \footnotesize
        \item Robot arm: joint torques; Locomotion: motor commands; Driving: steering angle + throttle.
        \item The set is \emph{uncountable} --- there is no ``list'' to enumerate or take $\arg\max_a Q(s,a)$ over.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.4em}
\centering\footnotesize\textcolor{red}{This lecture: how to do RL when actions are continuous.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{From huge \emph{discrete} spaces to \emph{continuous} control}
\vspace{1em}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Go, Dota, and StarCraft have \emph{enormous but still discrete} action sets --- you can (in principle) enumerate and $\arg\max$ over them.
    \vspace{0.4em}
    \item Robotics and physical control are \emph{continuous}: joint torques, steering angles, velocities. The action set is uncountable --- enumeration and $\arg\max_a Q(s,a)$ are no longer possible.
    \vspace{0.4em}
    \item[$\Rightarrow$] We need a method that learns a policy \emph{directly} over a continuous action space --- a first ``deep'' crack at this is \textbf{DDPG} \rref{5}.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
