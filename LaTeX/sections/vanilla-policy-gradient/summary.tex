\begin{frame}{Limitations of REINFORCE}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{High variance} in gradient estimates --- Monte Carlo returns are noisy.
        \item \textbf{Sample inefficiency} --- on-policy: each trajectory is used once and discarded.
        \item \textbf{Slow convergence} --- many samples needed before the signal-to-noise ratio is acceptable.
        \item \textbf{No credit assignment} across timesteps beyond the causality trick and discounting.
        \item Variance reduction techniques (baselines, advantage) help but do not fully solve these issues.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
    \item It can be hard to learn the exact Q-value for every (state, action) pair in high-dimensional state and action spaces.
    \item However, we can just learn a policy that maximizes the reward.
    \item We can use gradient ascent on policy parameters.
    \item However, this can suffer from high variance. Various strategies exist to tackle this: causality, discounting, baselines, and the advantage function.
    \item \textit{Next: Day 4 introduces Actor-Critic methods, which learn a critic $V_\phi$ alongside the policy to make advantage estimation practical.}
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
