\begin{frame}{Why Actor-Critic Helps}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Variance reduction vs REINFORCE:} the TD residual $\delta_t$ is a much lower-variance signal than REINFORCE's reward-to-go minus baseline, $R_t - b(s_t)$, because it bootstraps off $V_\phi$ rather than waiting for the full trajectory
    \item \textbf{Online updates:} no need to wait until the end of an episode --- the critic provides a training signal at every timestep
    \item \textbf{Bootstrapping:} propagating value estimates forward allows the agent to learn from partial trajectories and even in continuing (non-episodic) tasks
    \pause
    \item \textbf{Trade-off:} if $V_\phi$ is a bad critic, the TD residual is a biased advantage estimate --- the actor is being guided by a noisy critic
    \item Both networks must be trained together carefully; instability can arise if the critic lags the actor
    \item[$\Rightarrow$] So we have swapped REINFORCE's \emph{variance} problem for a \emph{bias} problem. Neither extreme is right --- the next section builds the dial between them.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
