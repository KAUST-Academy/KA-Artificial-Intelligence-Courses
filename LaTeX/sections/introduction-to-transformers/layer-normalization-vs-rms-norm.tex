\begin{frame}{Layer Normalization vs RMSNorm}
  \small
  \textbf{LayerNorm:} Normalizes across the feature dimension by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation.

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \textbf{RMSNorm:} Does not subtract the mean; it only re-scales the features by their root mean square (RMS).

  \vspace{1em}
  \begin{itemize}
      \item[$\blacktriangleright$] Simpler: one statistic per token instead of two, and no learned bias
      \item[$\blacktriangleright$] Faster: 7--64\% less running time depending on the model (Zhang \& Sennrich, 2019)
      \item[$\blacktriangleright$] Accuracy is comparable: on WMT14 English-German a Transformer scores 26.8 BLEU with RMSNorm against 26.6 with LayerNorm, at a 7--9\% speedup
      \item[$\blacktriangleright$] Adopted by LLaMA and the decoder-only models that followed it
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
