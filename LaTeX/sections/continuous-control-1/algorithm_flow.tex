% Slide 45b: soft target update equations (spelled out)
\begin{frame}{Method --- ``Soft'' target network update}
\vspace{0.5em}
DQN copies the online weights into the target network \emph{periodically} (a hard update every $C$ steps). DDPG instead lets the targets \emph{slowly track} the online networks every step:
\[
\theta^{Q'} \leftarrow \tau\, \theta^{Q} + (1-\tau)\, \theta^{Q'}
\qquad\qquad
\theta^{\mu'} \leftarrow \tau\, \theta^{\mu} + (1-\tau)\, \theta^{\mu'}
\]
\vspace{0.2em}
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\tau \ll 1$ (typically $\tau = 10^{-3}$): targets change very slowly.
    \item Constrains the TD target to move slowly $\Rightarrow$ greatly improves stability of learning, at the cost of slower value propagation.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% Slide 46b: exploration noise --- OU vs Gaussian
\begin{frame}{Method --- Exploration in a deterministic policy}
\vspace{0.4em}
A deterministic actor $\mu(s\mid\theta^{\mu})$ has \emph{no} exploration of its own. DDPG explores by adding noise to the behaviour policy:
\[
a_t = \mu(s_t \mid \theta^{\mu}) + \mathcal{N}_t
\]
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DDPG} \rref{5}: $\mathcal{N}_t$ from an \emph{Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process} \rref{12} --- temporally \emph{correlated} noise, well suited to physical control with inertia/momentum.
    \vspace{0.3em}
    \item \textbf{Later finding (TD3 \rref{7}):} uncorrelated zero-mean \emph{Gaussian} noise works just as well and is simpler --- now the common default.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
